CHARLES I, 1625-1649Crown against Parliament

Il percorso, passo per passo

1

DIVINE RIGHT

kings chosen by God, his

representatives on Earth;

absolute power;

total obedience expected

▼ then
2

TAXES AND CHURCH

taxes raised without the

consent of Parliament;

absolute obedience to the

Church of England

▼ but
3

PETITION OF RIGHT

1628: money for the war with

France only if the king accepts:

no taxes without Parliament;

no prison without a good reason

▼ still
4

WILLIAM LAUD

Archbishop of Canterbury;

High Church worship; persecutes

the Puritans (thousands go to

America); later executed

▼ so
5

PERSONAL RULE

1629: disagreements get worse;

Charles shuts Parliament and

rules as a despot for

11 years (until 1640)

▼ until
6

THE 1640 PARLIAMENTS

1640: money for a war with Scotland;

Short Parliament: a month; then

Long Parliament: refuses funds,

aims to reduce the king’s power

▼ really
7

CIVIL WAR 1642-49

Royalists (king, Anglican nobles)

v Roundheads (Parliament,

Puritans, middle class);

Ironsides win Marston Moor, Naseby

▼ ends in
8

1649

the king refuses to recognise

the court; Cromwell has the

death warrant signed;

beheaded in January 1649

▼ beware
9

COMMON MISTAKES

Royalists = Puritans (✗)

Short = Long Parliament (✗)

1628 = 1629 (✗)

king executed after Cromwell (✗)

→ Segui i riquadri in ordine, da 1 a 9: è un unico racconto, non percorsi diversi.

→ The king (1) wants power and taxes without Parliament (2); Laud (4) helps him.

→ After 11 years alone (5), the Long Parliament (6) goes against him.

→ The Civil War (7) ends with the king beheaded in January 1649 (8). Last, the common mistakes (9).

1

1649: REPUBLIC

England is proclaimed a

Commonwealth (a Republic);

▼ run by
2

RUMP PARLIAMENT

until 1653 England is ruled by

the House of Commons: only

supporters of the Republic

▼ until

1650: REACTION

the king’s execution causes a

Scotland; the Scottish revolt

▼ then
4

1653: PROTECTOR

Cromwell closes Parliament down;

he is appointed “Lord Protector

of the Realm”:

a military dictator

▼ rules
5

THREE COUNTRIES

Cromwell rules over England,

Ireland and Scotland

as a military dictator

▼ but
6

1655: RISING

in March another Royalist

rising breaks out; so Cromwell

starts to rule the country

through the army

▼ then
7

1658

Cromwell is succeeded by

his son Richard, who

abdicates 8 months later;

the Republic falls apart

▼ so
8

THE END

the Republic falls apart;

the monarchy returns in 1660

with Charles II

(the Restoration)

▼ beware
9

COMMON MISTAKES

Rump = House of Lords (✗)

Mappa 3 di 6 · John Milton

JOHN MILTON1608-1674: Puritan poet

Il percorso, passo per passo

1

LIFE

born in London in 1608, into a

Puritan family; St Paul’s School

and Cambridge; he studies classical

and several modern languages

▼ then
2

FIRST PERIOD

Italian poems: L’Allegro and

Il Penseroso (1631), after Petrarch;

Latin and English poems;

Defensio (1651): supports Cromwell

▼ then
3

SECOND PERIOD

numerous political pamphlets

and prose works;

Areopagitica (1644): defence of

freedom of speech, against censorship

▼ while
4

POLITICS

he supports Cromwell and the

Puritans during the Civil War;

he defends the execution

of Charles I; blind by 1651

▼ namely
5

LIBERTIES

Milton defends religious, civil

and domestic liberties;

freedom of the press, of speech;

Parliament; divorce

▼ then
6

THIRD PERIOD

marked by blindness and poverty;

he produces his masterpiece,

Paradise Lost (1667)

▼ then
7

1660

he falls from grace with the

Restoration of Charles II; he hides,

is briefly arrested; poverty;

1664-1674: he devotes himself to poetry

▼ then
8

1674

Milton dies. A famous line:

“The mind is its own place,

and in itself can make a heaven

of hell, a hell of heaven”

▼ beware
9

COMMON MISTAKES

blind in 1608 (✗)

Areopagitica = poem (✗)

died in 1667 (✗)

Royalist supporter (✗)

Connessioni chiave

→ Segui i riquadri in ordine, da 1 a 9: è un unico racconto, non percorsi diversi.

→ A Puritan (1) who supported Cromwell (4) and defended freedom of speech (3, 5).

→ Three periods: poems (2), prose (3), the masterpiece (6). The Restoration (7) brought him poverty.

→ He dies in 1674 (8). Last, the common mistakes (9).

Mappa 4 di 6 · Paradise Lost: the plot

PARADISE LOST: PLOTGenesis retold, 1667

Il percorso, passo per passo

1

CHAINED IN HELL

the poem begins with Satan

and his companion, Beelzebub,

chained to a lake of fire

in Hell

▼ then
2

THEY FREE THEMSELVES

they free themselves and want

to start another war

against God

▼ so
3

BEELZEBUB’S IDEA

Beelzebub suggests to corrupt

mankind, God’s beloved

new creation: so Satan

goes to Eden

▼ he goes
4

THE SERPENT

Satan, as a serpent, compliments

Eve on her beauty; she asks how

he learned to speak; he says he

ate the fruit of the Tree

▼ then
5

EVE AND ADAM EAT

Eve eats the fruit; Adam is

horrified, but eats too:

he prefers to be fallen with

her than to remain pure

▼ so
6

GOD KNOWS

God knows immediately about

their disobedience

and decides the punishment

▼ so
7

PUNISHMENT

God first punishes the serpent:

it must crawl on the ground;

Adam and Eve are condemned

to pain and death

▼ later
8

REDEMPTION

the archangel Michael shows

them the future of mankind;

comforted by a vision of

redemption, they leave Eden

▼ beware
9

COMMON MISTAKES

Adam eats first (✗)

Michael punishes (✗)

Satan stays in Hell (✗)

God learns it later (✗)

Connessioni chiave

→ Segui i riquadri in ordine, da 1 a 9: è un unico racconto, non percorsi diversi.

→ In Hell Satan (1) frees himself (2); Beelzebub’s idea (3) leads to the serpent (4).

→ Eve and Adam eat the fruit (5); God knows at once (6) and punishes them (7).

→ Michael shows a vision of redemption (8). Last, the common mistakes (9).

Mappa 5 di 6 · Paradise Lost: ideas

PARADISE LOST: IDEASthemes, universe, Satan, style

Il percorso, passo per passo

1

TWO PATHS

Adam and Eve’s disobedience, in

Satan’s rebellion against God;

Satan: sin, degradation, punishment

Man: repentance, hope of redemption

▼ within
2

FOUR REALMS

geography based on religion,

not on science: glorious Heaven

(above), dreadful Hell (below),

confusing Chaos, a young Earth

▼ namely
3

SATAN

refuses God’s superiority and

is banished to Hell; he says:

“Better to reign in Hell than

serve in Heaven”

▼ namely
4

THEMES

disobedience (Satan, Adam, Eve);

rebellion (Satan); redemption

through repentance; God’s

grace and mercy save humanity

▼ from
5

HIERARCHY

from close to far from God:

the Son, archangels and cherubs,

humans, animals,

devils (fallen angels)

▼ first
6

HEROIC, THEN LOW

God is always more powerful, but

Milton respects Satan’s courage;

at first heroic, in the end

degraded, dominated by vanity

▼ namely
7

STYLE

a complex work for highly-educated

readers: obscure allusions,

archaic vocabulary, complex

grammar, extended similes

▼ namely
8

METER

blank verse: unrhymed iambic

pentameters, as used by Marlowe

and Shakespeare; Milton thought

rhyme unsuitable for his subject

▼ beware
9

COMMON MISTAKES

Satan chooses redemption (✗)

science-based universe (✗)

rhymed verse (✗)

Adam and Eve are damned (✗)

Connessioni chiave

→ Segui i riquadri in ordine, da 1 a 9: è un unico racconto, non percorsi diversi.

→ Two moral paths (1): Satan chooses damnation; Adam and Eve, redemption.

→ The universe has four realms (2) and a hierarchy by closeness to God (5).

→ Satan (3, 6) looks heroic, then degraded; style (7) is complex; blank verse (8). Last, the mistakes (9).
